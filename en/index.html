#How it works

Untangles the fight from both ends

You tell the bot what happened in your own words, tears or fury included. Your partner gets a link and tells the same story from their side. The bot compares both versions using the Gottman method, the way family therapists do, minus the jargon and the two-week waiting list. It starts by asking who you are to each other: a couple, parent and child, friends or coworkers.

Not your lawyer, not a judge

A lawyer takes your side, a judge decides who's guilty. After either, making up only gets harder. Instead, the bot explains what hurt you and what was going on with the person across from you. After that you want to make up, not to win.

Finds the words when you have none

It lays out what happened and what you're missing right now: an apology, or simply being heard. It gives you one small step for today. And before you send an angry message, show it to the bot: it rewrites it so you get heard instead of a slammed door.

You close the chapter together

Once you've both cooled down, each of you writes where you landed, and the bot turns it into a shared summary. The next day it checks whether you talked. If the same topic comes up a third time, it says so plainly, so you fix the cause, not the trigger.

What you tell the bot stays with the bot

Write it as it is, caps lock included. Your partner has their own chat, and your words never end up there, not even paraphrased. They join through your link in a minute. If they're not ready yet, start without them: the "figure it out myself" mode exists for exactly that.

He came home at eleven again and went straight to his phone. I waited all evening, and he doesn't care.
It sounds like it isn't about the clock. What stings is that the evening you were waiting for went by without you. As if you weren't in it.
When you're ready, open not with "you don't care" but with "I waited for you all evening, and I felt lonely." That way he hears you, not an accusation.

#How it's different from ChatGPT

ChatGPT is always on your side. That's why it doesn't help.

A regular AI only hears you, so it always agrees. Mediator reads both versions, so it can't just nod along: its job is to get the two of you to finally hear each other.

Frequently asked questions

Will my partner see my messages?

No. Only the bot reads them. It won't show or retell them to your partner, not even close to the text. What it says about you is gentle and never a quote: not "here's what was said" but "it seems they feel lonely and want to be heard."

What if my partner refuses to take part?

Start without them. The bot helps you understand what hurt and prepare for the conversation. You can invite your partner later, when they're ready, even a week from now.

Will the bot say who's right?

No. "Who's right" usually breeds round two, not peace. Instead the bot explains what's going on with each of you. Often that alone lets the anger go.

Is it a replacement for therapy?

No. The bot helps you get through one specific fight: understand what happened and decide to talk. If the problem runs deeper or there's abuse in the relationship, it says so plainly, that you need a real professional, and points you to help.

How much does it cost and how do I pay?

One analysis a month is free. After that it's a subscription: 150 Telegram Stars for 7 days or 300 Stars for 30 days. Stars are bought in a couple of taps inside Telegram with a card from any country. Only one of you pays; your partner joins for free.

Is it only for couples?

No, it works for any two people: parents, friends, coworkers. Just pick the relationship type at the start.
